## 1. The code is right, and now it is provable

`research/gtsam_poc/verify_kf_identity.py`

For a linear-Gaussian chain the fixed-lag MAP estimate of the newest state IS
the Kalman filter estimate. So freezing every linearization where a filter
would put it (`--norelin`) and dropping the robust kernel (`--norobust`) leaves
the implementation no freedom: it must reproduce the filter.

    max relative difference, all 37 states    1.1e-06
    max horizontal position difference        8.5 nanometres

over 600 graph states. The Monte Carlo says the same without being asked: the
`--norelin --norobust` configuration scores a DRMS ratio of exactly 1.000
against the filter with 0 wins out of 30 seeds at both initial-error scales.

This is a sharper test than any accuracy comparison because it has a known
right answer. Put it in the paper. It closes the "is this a porting bug"
question permanently.

## 2. The causal accuracy claim does not survive

`research/gtsam_poc/mc_1007.py`, `mc_table.py`, `mc_decomp.py`

A Monte Carlo is possible on recorded data after all. The error state obeys
x_{s+1} = Phi x_s + w, so adding a homogeneous solution to it is still a valid
error trajectory: draw d ~ N(0, P0) over the seventeen inertial states,
propagate with the exported Phi, subtract its position components from the INS
track. Measurement, map, Tolles-Lawson regressor and recorded process noise are
all untouched. Nothing is fabricated. Common random numbers across estimators
make the per-seed RATIO the statistic.

Against a plain EKF, kernel off on both sides, whole segment scored:

    scale 0.1 (realistic)   causal 0.970 30/30    smoothed 0.250 30/30
    scale 0.3               causal 0.988 19/30    smoothed 0.241 30/30
    scale 1.0 (the prior)   causal 0.989 15/30    smoothed 0.203 30/30
    scale 3.0 (cold start)  causal 0.896 27/30    smoothed 0.268 27/30

Decomposition, each knob isolated by its own control at fixed seeds:

    robust kernel, given to the filter    0.977 at scale 0.1, 0.999 at 3.0
    relinearization, kernel-free          0.970 at scale 0.1, 0.896 at 3.0
    smoothing                             a linear fixed-lag smoother gets
                                          0.259, the graph 0.250

And against an iterated EKF, which fixes its own linearization:

    scale 0.1   IEKF/EKF 0.982    ours/IEKF 0.988 27/30
    scale 3.0   IEKF/EKF 0.953    ours/IEKF 0.940 27/30  worst seed 3.71

So: at a realistic initial error the graph is worth one to three percent over
any baseline. The 4x on the smoothed output is real but a linear smoother gets
essentially all of it. The only place the formulation earns its keep is a cold
start several times the assumed prior, where 6% survives an IEKF at p = 8e-6 --
with a tail that runs 3.71x the wrong way on the worst seed.

Read `mc_table.py`'s docstring before quoting any of this. Two numbers in it
are traps and are labelled as such: the 30/30 at scale 0.1 is not thirty
independent facts (scatter is 58% of the effect, effective n is nearer one),
and the 0.713 smoothed ratio at scale 3.0 sits on 17/30 at p = 0.58.

## 4. Why FGO ties an EKF here, mechanically

Worth writing into Section I, because it turns a weakness into an explanation.

Our graph carries `Vector` variables and `CustomFactor`s. The dynamics factor
is `e = xb - Phi @ xa` with Phi precomputed in Julia, so the INS mechanization
was linearized OUTSIDE the graph and the raw IMU never enters it. The only
nonlinearity left is the map interpolation, and an anomaly map is smooth over
tens of metres of position error. The graph is an error-state EKF in graph
clothing -- which is exactly why item 1 measures 8.5 nanometres.

The channels through which FGO beats a filter are loop closure, delayed and
out-of-order measurements, structural reparameterization, global robust
handling, and relinearization under genuine nonlinearity. A single-pass
straight flight line with synchronous scalar measurements has none of the first
four and almost none of the fifth.

Park's IJCAS TRN paper is the useful comparison and it is not an accuracy
argument either: the title is "Sparse Instantiation of Bias Nodes". The graph
buys a parameterization a filter's state vector cannot express. Our analogue is
the 19-dimensional time-varying compensation against their one scalar, and the
manuscript already says so.

Sparse beta instantiation was tried and is negative on the easy channel:
period 30/60/150/300/0 s gives 9.98/7.64/8.75/7.36/7.80 smoothed against a 7.79
control, no trend, scatter from node placement wider than any gap. Only the
hard cases (Mag 4, and the two lines trailing a causal filter) are untested,
and they need full-line data.

## 5. The result that came out of the IMU question

`research/observability_audit.py`

The question was whether the map could calibrate the IMU. On recorded data, no:
accelerometer bias posterior is 0.992 of its prior (0.1 dB) and gyro bias is
worse than its prior, while velocity gets 12 to 18 dB and tilt 8 to 10.

The reason is structural. Bias enters as `F[4:6,12:14] = Cnb` and
`F[7:9,15:17] = -Cnb`, so a constant body-frame bias projects into NED along a
direction fixed by the attitude; hold the attitude and it is indistinguishable
from the tilt it shares a column with. Every SGL flight LINE is straight, so
the recorded data cannot answer this. A synthetic trajectory over the real map
can. `--validate` reproduces the recorded straight-line figures from the real
exported Phi and A before any synthetic number is believed.

Dropping the prior and inverting the observability Gramian -- what the map
ALONE would know:

    trajectory          accel bx      vs prior     gyro bx        vs prior
    straight   600 s    1.6e4 m/s2    6.6e7        0.074 rad/s    1e7
    turn 30    600 s    1811          7.4e6        10.7           1.5e9
    turn 30   3600 s      16.0        6.5e4        0.096          1.3e7
    racetrack 3600 s       0.0028     11           1.0e-6         1.4e2

The lever is the RACETRACK, not the turn. A continuous turn stays in one place
and covers no map; alternating legs translate and rotate at once. Same hour,
six thousand times the information.

In instrument units the racetrack hour gives **286 ug and 0.21 deg/hr from the
map alone**. Against the navigation-grade INS SGL flies (P0 assumes 25 ug and
0.0015 deg/hr) that loses by two orders. Against tactical it is comparable to
5x better; against MEMS it wins by one to two orders.

So: a magnetic anomaly map can calibrate a cheap IMU in flight, provided the
trajectory turns. Nobody has seen it because the public benchmark flies a
navigation-grade INS down straight lines -- the two worst conditions at once.
This is estimator-agnostic and does not touch the FGO-versus-EKF argument at
all.

Linear observability for a synthetic trajectory over a real map. It bounds what
is worth attempting; it does not demonstrate it.
